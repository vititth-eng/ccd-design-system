Chart palette — fixing 1 / 6 / 7

Four of the seven stay exactly as picked: rose, teal, orange, violet. Three move. Colour-blind separation was measured and deliberately dropped as a constraint — everything below is normal vision. Grounds are hardcoded here on purpose; what is under test is which colours sit too close to each other, not the theming system.

3 · chart-6 — yellow has no good answer, and that is the finding

A yellow bright enough to look yellow is invisible on white (1.51:1); a yellow dark enough to see is olive — the same wall --cau-strong hit this morning. And status already owns amber. So the real question is whether the chart tier should claim yellow at all. Four candidates, pick by eye:

A keep #F4CF4A — brightest, most yours; invisible on white, 8.1 from caution.
B lime #B0C431 — clears caution (17.2); still only 1.95:1 on white and drifts toward the status green.
C brown #A2673A — the pick, and the only option that fixes both problems at once: 4.63:1 on white, 3.40:1 on the dark card, 11.5 from cau-strong, 20.6 from series 4. Cost: it is the one earthy colour in a bright palette. A darker #995E33 scored better on paper but sat at exactly 3.00:1 on the dark card — on the bar with no margin, so it was dropped.
D magenta #D0399E — 4.40:1, miles from every status hue; but 16.5 from series 2 rose, so the set gains a second pink.

5 · The whole set — bars, then 2px lines

Thin lines are the real test: 2px of colour with nothing around it. Slot 6 shown as brown (C) here, since it scored best — say the word and I'll swap in lime or magenta.

6 · Most charts want one hue, not seven

The pattern the doctrine now names. Subject in chart-1, context in chart-7 grey. Compare it against the same data wearing all seven — this is what "choose by the data" buys you.
