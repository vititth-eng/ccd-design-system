The peach sits at exactly the same lightness step as the cream it replaces, so the family keeps its even spacing. Only the hue moves — from yellow to orange, which is what stops it fighting an orange strong.

Why 3:1 is the right bar here, not a shortcut. WCAG asks 4.5:1 of body text and 3:1 of icons and graphics. Today --cau-strong has exactly one use — the glyph in the warning state icon — so 3:1 is the bar it actually has to clear. Holding it to 4.5:1 is what dragged its hue to olive in the first place: to get that dark on a pale tint, the colour has to give up its orange.

The one thing this trades away. If anyone later sets body copy in --cau-strong, it will fail AA at 3.40:1. That needs to be written into the token comment as a rule — glyphs and marks only, never a paragraph — or the constraint is lost the first time someone reaches for it.
