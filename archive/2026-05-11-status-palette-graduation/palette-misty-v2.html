Contrast read

  • Positive olive on paper is the most readable. On tint and wash it's softer but legible — olive sits comfortably in its own pastel.
  • Caution amber reads well on paper. On tint it loses some punch (same hue family bg). On wash it's strong.
  • Negative rust reads strongest. Doesn't fight any of the surfaces.
  • Implication: if we ship wash-only, delta text and small status labels stay legible. If we keep tint for some cases, caution+amber text on tint+amber is the weakest pair — flag it as an anti-pattern.

  • Pill clarity. Which dot reads as distinct from the bg, not blurred into it?

  • Family cohesion. Compare your favorite to the pos/neg pills above (still tinted). Does it still look like a sibling, or like an outlier?
  • The clean rule: if C4 (wash) wins, the answer is "tint for the strong pairs, wash for the weak ones." If C2/C3 wins, "shift the tint hexes — same system, cleaner pair." If C1 wins, "punching the dot is enough."

What to look for

  • Pill busyness. Look at the 9-pill row. Does v2 feel anxious vs v1? If yes, the cost of punching is real. If no, punching is free.
