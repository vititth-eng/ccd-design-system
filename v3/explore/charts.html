Chart colour — CCD categorical vs shadcn sequential

shadcn ships chart-1…5 as a single hue stepping down in lightness. CCD ships seven distinct hues. These are not two takes on the same thing — they answer different questions, and swapping them silently breaks charts.

1 · The two palettes

CCD — 7 categorical hues

Answers “which one is this”. Identity only; the colour means nothing beyond “a different one”.

shadcn blue — 5 sequential steps

Answers “how much”. The magnitude lives in the lightness.

2 · Used categorically — seven plants, no order between them

Same data, same labels. On the right the reader sees a ranking that is not in the data: the lightness ramp says “these are ordered”, and no legend takes that back.

CCD palette

Seven identities, no implied order. Correct use.

shadcn palette

Reads as ranked light-to-dark, and runs out at five. Wrong tool for this question.

3 · Used sequentially — one measure, low to high

Reverse the question and the verdicts reverse with it. This is the case CCD has never built — v2's own note says sequential is unsized and warns against inventing values in a mock.

CCD palette

Seven unrelated hues for one measure. The magnitude is invisible.

shadcn palette

Lightness carries the size of the number. Correct use — and the gap in our set.

4 · The CCD palette, measured

Contrast against white, and separation from its neighbours under normal vision and deuteranopia (the most common colour-vision deficiency). A fill under 3:1 is a shape you cannot locate; two colours under ΔE 10 are the same colour to most people.
