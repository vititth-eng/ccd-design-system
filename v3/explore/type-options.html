Question 1

How much air between lines of Thai?

Thai stacks tone marks above and vowels below, so it needs more room than English at the same size. v2 uses 1.6. Current Thai guidance says 1.8. Read both — the question is whether A feels cramped.

Question 2

How wide should a paragraph run?

v2 never set a limit, so text runs as wide as its container. Long lines make the eye lose its place on the return sweep — the comfortable band is 50–75 characters. Measured live in this window: 2A = 83, 2B = 64, 2C = 82. Note that 2A and 2C look nearly identical here, which is the finding: at this width Tailwind’s “prose” limit is barely constraining anything. On a 1366 laptop 2A runs wider still.

Question 3

Should headings sit closer to their own text?

A heading belongs to what comes after it. In A the gaps are round numbers (32 above a heading, 16 below). In B they are odd numbers chosen so the gaps look even — because every line of text carries invisible padding that the round numbers don’t account for. Look at whether each heading reads as attached to its paragraph or floating between two.

Question 4

Do two big numbers stacked have enough room?

At 1.15 the line box is slightly smaller than the letters themselves, so stacked numbers nearly touch. 1.2 gives them their own space. Small difference — look at the gap between the two rows.
