v3 — the scale standard

Two ramps and a grey. Blue is positive, rose is negative, grey is neutral or unhighlighted. Every rating scale is built from those, so a 4-point and a 7-point survey read as the same system.

the ingredients

Two ramps, one grey

Intensity lives in lightness: the strongest opinion is darkest, the mildest is palest. Hue only says which direction.

the standard

Symmetric scales — equal options each side

Even counts are the easy case: split down the middle, darkest at each extreme. Odd counts need a true centre, and that centre is grey — not a blend of blue and rose, which is mud. Grey already means “neither / unhighlighted”, which is exactly what a midpoint is.

the harder case

Asymmetric scales — the options aren't balanced

the catch, stated up front

A diverging scale carries nothing in greyscale

Because the two sides are deliberately symmetric, matching steps have matching lightness. Measured on these ramps:

scaleluminance, left to right
2-point0.275 · 0.276blue and rose are luminance twins
4-point0.12 · 0.51 · 0.493 · 0.11the two mild steps mirror
7-point0.12 · 0.271 · 0.51 · 0.577 · 0.493 · 0.255 · 0.11symmetric around grey

This is not a flaw in the ramps — it is what “diverging” means. Every diverging scale in every system has it. The consequence is the rule: order and labels carry a rating scale; colour is the reinforcement. Keep the sequence fixed (most positive always at one end), always label the ends, and never rely on hue alone to say which side a segment is on. A sequential ramp does not have this problem — its lightness is monotonic, so it survives greyscale intact.
